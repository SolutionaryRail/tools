Methods and sources
  • Freight volumes. Tons and trip lengths come from the Freight Analysis Framework version 5 (FAF5, FHWA and Oak Ridge National Laboratory), 2022. Each origin-to-destination flow is routed over the FAF5 road network by shortest travel time. These are modeled estimates, not traffic counts.
  • Delaware miles only. Ton-miles count only the miles driven on Delaware roads (and, for rail, an assumed equal number of Delaware miles), so effects in other states are not counted here and will not be counted twice when other states are added.
  • Likely a floor. On roads matched to FHWA’s 2025 traffic counts (HPMS), the modeled freight trucks are about 12% of counted trucks (about 11% on interstates). Counted trucks include all traffic, such as local trips and trucks not hauling modeled freight. The “Scale roads to counted trucks” switch (under More options) raises the road layer to match the counts; the cost estimates always use modeled freight.
  • External costs. Per-ton-mile costs are the midpoints of the ranges in CBO Working Paper 2015-03, Table 1, adjusted from 2014 to 2025 dollars with the PCE price index. They cover pavement damage, congestion, accidents, and particulate and nitrogen-oxide emissions, include empty return trips, and are net of taxes and fees. Carbon dioxide is priced separately at Washington’s state social cost of carbon (about $102 per metric ton in 2025 dollars, from the Utilities and Transportation Commission’s table under WAC 194-40-100), multiplied by EPA’s average emissions factors of 0.203 kg CO2 per truck ton-mile and 0.022 kg per rail ton-mile (EPA GHG Emission Factors Hub). Congestion costs are a national average and are likely understated on busy urban roads.
  • Savings. The saving is the truck cost minus the rail cost for the same ton-miles, times the share you choose to shift. It assumes the freight can move by rail and does not include the cost of building capacity.
  • Freight passing through. About 54% of the truck ton-miles on Delaware roads (93% on trips of 500+ miles) are for freight that neither starts nor ends in Delaware. The FAF data do not list roads for each shipment, so this tool routes every origin-destination pair over the FAF highway network by shortest path and keeps the Delaware share, then scales each road to the FAF road assignment. Roads in other states and Canada are not drawn.
  • Boundaries. U.S. Census Bureau 2025 cartographic boundary files (counties, 119th Congress districts, state legislative districts, reservations). Rail lines: Bureau of Transportation Statistics, National Transportation Atlas Database. Delaware’s Senate and House districts are different maps.
  • Not yet included. Tire and brake wear, noise, and other costs outside the CBO estimates; freight on local roads; and out-of-service rail lines.
